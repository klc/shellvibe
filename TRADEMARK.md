# Trademark policy

**ShellVibe™** — the name, the logo, and the app icons — identifies software
published by Mustafa Kılıç. The marks are claimed through use; registration is
being pursued and this page will be updated when it completes.

The code licence and the brand are deliberately separate things. Anyone may
take the code. Nobody may take the name. That separation is what makes it safe
to publish the source at all: a fork can compete on merit, but it cannot arrive
in a user's app store wearing our identity.

## Covered by this policy, not by the licence

Everything in [`assets/brand/`](assets/brand/) — the master icon and its
platform variants — plus the word **ShellVibe**, any logo or wordmark derived
from it, and confusingly similar names such as `ShellVibes`, `Shell-Vibe` or
`ShelVibe`.

[`LICENSE`](LICENSE) grants no rights in any of these. Its Trademarks clause is
explicit: apart from identifying us as the origin of the software, the licence
gives you no right to use our marks.

## What you may do without asking

- Say that your work is based on, forked from, derived from, or compatible with
  ShellVibe — in prose, factually, without styling it as an endorsement.
- Link to the project, and use the name when discussing, reviewing, teaching or
  criticising it.
- Keep the copyright and licence notices in the source you redistribute. Those
  are required, not optional.
- Use the marks in the ordinary way journalism and commentary do: nominative
  use is not something a trademark can prohibit.

## What requires written permission

- Distributing a build of ShellVibe, modified or not, under the ShellVibe name
  or with its logo or icons. **This is the case that matters.** A fork you ship
  needs its own name, its own icon, and its own app-store listing identity.
- Naming a product, service, company, domain, package or app-store listing in a
  way that a user could mistake for ours.
- Using the marks on merchandise, or in a way that implies partnership,
  sponsorship or endorsement.

## Fork policy, in one sentence

Fork it, change it, sell it within the licence's Permitted Purpose — but ship
it under your own name and your own icon.

If you are unsure whether what you have in mind needs permission, ask before
you publish rather than after: **licensing@shellvibe.dev**. Permission is
frequently given; unwinding a released app is what nobody wants.

## After the Apache 2.0 conversion

[`LICENSING.md`](LICENSING.md) explains that each release becomes Apache 2.0
two years on. That conversion concerns the copyright licence only. Trademark
rights are separate, run on their own terms, and do not lapse with it — a
two-year-old release is Apache 2.0 code, still not permission to call it
ShellVibe.
